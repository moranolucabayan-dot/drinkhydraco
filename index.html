The science

Electrolytes & recovery, simply

What actually happens in your body when you sweat and train hard — and where RestoreHydrapro fits in.

01 Electrolytes

What they are

Electrolytes are minerals — sodium, potassium and magnesium among them — that carry a tiny electric charge once dissolved in water. That charge is how your body moves water in and out of cells, sends nerve signals and makes muscles contract and relax.

What sweat takes

Sweat is mostly water, but it carries electrolytes out with it — mostly sodium. In hard training you can sweat well over a litre an hour, and each litre carries roughly 0.2 to 2 g of sodium — it varies a lot from person to person.

  • 0.2–2 g sodium
  • some potassium
  • trace magnesium
  • per litre of sweat — varies by person

Sweat sodium varies widely between people — roughly 10–90 mmol (about 0.2–2 g) per litre — depending on how much the sweat gland reabsorbs before sweat reaches the skin. Baker & Wolfe, 2020, European Journal of Applied Physiology

Why a little sugar helps

Your gut absorbs sodium fastest when a little glucose comes with it: one transporter carries the two in together, and water follows. It’s the same principle behind the oral rehydration solutions used in medicine — which is why RestoreHydrapro has 3 g of cane sugar, not zero and not 30.

02 Recovery

What happens after hard training

Hard or unfamiliar exercise causes tiny tears in muscle fibres and a burst of oxidative stress — reactive molecules made during intense effort. Inflammation follows as the body repairs, and that’s the stiffness you feel the next day: delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS), which typically peaks one to three days after the workout.

Where the fruit comes in

Tart cherry and pomegranate are rich in polyphenols — plant compounds such as anthocyanins that act as antioxidants. They’re the two fruits most studied for soreness and strength recovery, and both are in every stick.

a polyphenol ring, the antioxidant part

A meta-analysis of 14 trials found tart cherry gave a small reduction in soreness and a moderate improvement in strength recovery. Hill et al., 2021, International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism

Why hydration matters too

Starting a session already dehydrated cut endurance performance by about 2.4% on average in a 2020 review of 15 studies — and what you lose today carries into tomorrow’s session. Rehydrating with fluid and sodium helps your body keep the water instead of losing it straight back.

−2.4%endurance performance when starting exercise dehydrated

Across 15 studies, starting aerobic exercise hypohydrated lowered performance by 2.4% and peak oxygen uptake by 2.4%. Deshayes, Jeker & Goulet, 2020, Sports Medicine

01

What is a probiotic?

Your gut is home to trillions of bacteria — the gut microbiome. A probiotic is a live microbe that, taken in a large enough amount, has a measurable benefit for the person taking it. That’s the scientific definition, not a marketing one.

“Live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host.” International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics (ISAPP), 2022 guide for health professionals

02

Why this one survives

Most probiotics are fragile: heat, air, time on a shelf and stomach acid kill many of them before they reach your intestine. B. coagulans is different — it’s a spore-former. In the dry powder it waits as a dormant spore inside a hard protective coat, like a seed.

In lab tests, spores of the B. coagulans strain CGI314 withstood stomach-strength acid (pH 3), bile salts and heat up to 90 °C — the conditions that kill many other probiotics. Mazhar et al., 2024, Frontiers in Microbiology

03

What it does in your gut

  1. Stomach. The spore’s coat carries it through stomach acid.
  2. Small intestine. Warmth, moisture and nutrients “wake” it up: the spore germinates back into an active cell.
  3. Active. Like yogurt cultures, it makes lactic acid — it was once even named Lactobacillus sporogenes.
  4. Passing through. It doesn’t move in for good; it works on the way through, which is why probiotics are taken regularly.

The honest fine print

Probiotic effects depend on the exact strain and the dose, and the studies above are small, some are lab or industry-funded, and each used a specific strain — results for one strain don’t automatically carry over to another. CFU (colony-forming units) counts live cells; Hydrapro carries over 1 billion per stick. In 2020 scientists renamed the species Weizmannia coagulans, so newer papers use that name.
